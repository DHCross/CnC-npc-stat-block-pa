Often the inn and the local temple were the only non-dungeon environments ever encountered by those wide-eyed players.\n\nOf course, in a more developed campaign, the inn plays a wider role than just a\n\nplace to recuperate hit points. These establishments are a mainstay of the caravan systems that tend to extend across most civilized game worlds. Transporters of goods and people often maintain contractual relationships with good quality inns across the country, allowing their employees and passengers to be assured of at least a minimal degree of comfort along the way. Very large corporations may actually open their own way stations at which travelers can stop, change horses, and rest in security.\n\nRoadside inns dot the various major travel routes of the world, some luxurious and some more spartan. Either way, the inn fills an important niche for all that travel.\n\nInns and hostels are not confined to major thoroughfares of course. Small establishments offer comfort to travelers throughout the civilized lands, and some even spring up on the frontiers of the wilds. In territories where there are active bands of hostiles, humanoid or otherwise, innkeepers may seek informal truces or alliances to ensure the safety of their guests.\n\nMore than just a venue for shelter and food, the inn also offers an excellent venue for gathering information, networking, and other forms of social interaction. A variety of services tend to be available at rural inns. Some will also function as trading posts, or as informal messenger services.
