{'header': '### Role in the Campaign', 'content': '\nIt goes without saying that in regions beset by monsters and maniacal mages, gaols and prisons are rarely places of much comfort. Indeed, they generally serve as places to shelve inconvenient persons or those whose misdeeds are not quite serious enough to warrant execution in one of its many gruesome forms. While many nobles maintain their own dungeons (as do some temples and other organizations eager to keep their own malefactors under wraps), local chartered municipal authorities are often responsible for the penal system within the town limits.\n\nGenerally small and unpleasant places, the gaols in such towns are rarely designed to hold criminals for long periods. Often they serve to restrain dangerous or deranged\n\nindividuals until they can be dealt with more permanently, or to hold suspected lawbreakers while investigations are made. Sometimes these investigations require the use of the implements of torture commonly found in such gaols. Depending upon the moral compass of the authorities, the local dungeon and torture chamber may be horrific or simply dreadful.\n\nUnfortunately for adventurers and other roving strangers, gaols sometimes serve another purpose – that of income generation. Some small-town governments make a decent amount of money from fines extracted from outsiders who fall afoul of any of a multitude of local bylaws. Naturally, the authorities will generally avoid antagonizing any individuals or groups capable of violently resisting such extortion.\n\nThe treatment of inmates will depend very much upon the prevailing moral authority in charge of the gaol. It is important to keep in mind that in many societies, the concept of “innocent until proven guilty” is replaced by “if you weren’t guilty, you wouldn’t be in gaol”. Characters seeking to exercise what they think to be their “rights” may be in for a shock, as our more modern expectations of the rights of prisoners are unlikely to be met.\n\nRegardless of the relative harshness of the legal system, it is safe to assume that even the more humane facilities will be dank, dark, unpleasant places in which no one should seek to tarry. Gaols tend to be infested with vermin (both guards and inmates) as well as bugs, rodents, and other creepy crawlies.\n\n'}
