{'header': '## Noble Manor House', 'content': '\n**Role in the Campaign**\n\nA staple of many fantasy campaigns is the local Baron or manorial Lord who provides leadership for a rural area. Whether the setting is strictly feudal, with convoluted patterns of subinfeudation and fealty, or whether the local political and social system is more loosely defined, the local Lord is often a patron, ally, or foeman of the stalwart adventuring party.\n\nGenerally holding sway over a relatively small region populated by peasant farmers and perhaps a small village and local mill, the Lord is generally at the bottom of the nobility food chain. Manorial Lords usually owe allegiance to a more powerful noble and enjoy a variety of legal and customary powers and privileges. Though vastly better off than the peasants who farm their land, these nobles rarely live lives of ease and luxury.\n\nDepending upon where the manor is within the realm, the manor house may be a simple fortified stone keep or an elaborate country house with extensive gardens and expensive fixtures. Obviously manor in less secure regions will require more martial trappings, often being surrounded by walls or at least a wooden or dirt palisade. Motte and bailey keeps are often used in frontier regions as manorial seats as they provide simple yet effect protection for the Lord and his forces.\n\nIn more civilized settings, the manor house has evolved into more of a mansion. Often surrounded by meticulously planned an maintained grounds, with a variety of\n\ngardens, fountains, and statuary, these homes are clear displays of the wealth and privilege of their owners. They tend to have large staffs of servants, guards, and assorted hangers-on, and often function as small communities. The staff is often quite dedicated and status conscious, with a well-treated staff being a source of great support to the Lord, as they recognize their privileged role in serving him.\n\nLocal subjects of the manorial ruler are sometimes invited to annual feasts or festivals held on the grounds of the manor house. Often the harvest is celebrates, or else a particularly important religious holiday is observed with a day of games and merriment sponsored by the Lord.\n\nOf course, not all manorial Lords are benevolent. Some are harsh masters, keeping their people in servitude and misery. The manor houses of such masters are naturally less jolly places, with fewer gardens and more gibbets on the grounds. In such cases, the manor house will have more guards and better defences, just in case the rabble decides to rise up.\n\n'}
