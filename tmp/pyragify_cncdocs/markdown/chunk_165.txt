One thing most such places have in common, however, is that they are built specifically for the activity in question and, beyond simple gathering- places, are not suitable for much else. For example, the walls in the entryway of a theater might be painted with scenes from popular plays; a small park might be laid out to enhance the illusion that visitors are in a sylvan area rather than a city; or a domed room in a museum might be designed to make visitors feel as if they were underwater or under a night sky.\n\nDedicated entertainment venues, which are expensive to build and maintain and require a large population base to support, are usually characteristic of communities of large town size or bigger.
