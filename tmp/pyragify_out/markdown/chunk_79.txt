In most fantasy worlds, such associations come into being in the form of guilds of wizards. Just as carpenters or innkeepers recognize the value in banding together, so too does it make sense for the magical community to pool its resources to better the welfare of its members.\n\nMagic Guilds vary considerably from place to place and time to time. In some cases, where mages are generally accepted and respected members of society, the guilds serve simply as social clubs. Gatherings are held regularly to allow members a private, secure location to relax, share experiences, and compare notes. These guilds often contain libraries of rare books and scrolls and provide simple services such as research to members for a nominal fee. One city might have several such guilds, similar to the clubs that nobles set up as exclusive gathering places.\n\nIn other situations, guilds of mages can be grim, controlling organizations. Some guilds license the use of magic, striking against any non-members who dare to delve into the arcane arts without official sanction. These guilds generally try and regulate the teaching and use of magic, either because they jealously guard these secrets to increase their own power, or because they legitimately fear the havoc that could be wreaked by unschooled and undisciplined delving into forbidden arts.\n\nIn either case, the local Magic Guild is often the best place to seek advice, training, or assistance in all matter magical. Whether the need is for the identification of an obscure artifact pulled from an ancient tomb, or for the deciphering of a particularly vexing scroll, or the casting of particular magics, one would be well advised to seek out the guild for help. At the very least, the guild should be able to direct the adventurer to an appropriate practitioner of the arts.\n\nWithin the broader community, the Guild is often viewed with awe and not a small amount of suspicion. Even in settings where magic is commonplace, it will be viewed with trepidation by the simple folk who cannot use it. Though vastly better off than the peasants who farm their land, these nobles rarely live lives of ease and luxury.\n\nDepending upon where the manor is within the realm, the manor house may be a simple fortified stone keep or an elaborate country house with extensive gardens and expensive fixtures. Obviously manor in less secure regions will require more martial trappings, often being surrounded by walls or at least a wooden or dirt palisade. Motte and bailey keeps are often used in frontier regions as manorial seats as they provide simple yet effect protection for the Lord and his forces.\n\nIn more civilized settings, the manor house has evolved into more of a mansion.
