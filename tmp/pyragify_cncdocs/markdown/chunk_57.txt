In traditional ancient, medieval, or Renaissance urban areas, many craftsmen live and work in townhouses or other typical homes (although certain trades may require purpose-built structures). Certain tasks, however, require specialized equipment that no individual can do without.\n\nSecurity in these workshops is usually modest. Most craftsmen rely on sturdy doors, strong locks, and—where available—the vigilance of the city watch. While some armories produce a wide variety of armor types, most specialize in one or a few styles. Typical equipment found in an armory includes metalworking tools, leather aprons, anvils, forges, bellows—and, if technology permits, hydraulic hammers and blast furnaces.\n\nDepending on its size, an armory is usually run by at least one master armorer, who in turn may employ one or more apprentices and unskilled helpers. These artisans are adept at laboring long hours over hot forges, transforming raw metal into scales, plates, and other components; they work skillfully with small parts like chain links and rivets; and they combine these elements into finished sets of standard or custom armor.
