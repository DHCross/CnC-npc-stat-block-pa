Consider making it necessary for adventurers to seek out a master craftsman to have their new-found magical plate armor altered. Make this particularly nerve- wracking by insisting that characters leave the armor with the smith for several weeks. Choosing armor types from a radically different historical period or culture can make this a particularly powerful plot device. A race of mysterious goblinoids wearing an unknown style of laminated wooden armor is more interesting than yet another goblin in leather togs.\n\nOther possible reasons for visiting an armorer include maintenance and repair, commissioning of particularly extravagant items (solid gold breastplates etc), or appraising or selling loot acquired from the latest dungeon crawl. {'header': '### Role in the Campaign', 'content': '\nWithin most organized settlements above a certain size, there exists a force of some kind dedicated to either keeping order, or maintaining oppression. These guardsmen or constables serve a necessary function, for in their absence the resulting anarchy would prevent the social order necessary for the establishment and flourishing of a city. Neither particularly lucrative nor glamorous, the City Watch attracts either those truly dedicated to service, those with skill at arms but down-on-their-luck, or those seeking an excuse to exert their will over others and indulge their sadistic tendencies.\n\nSmaller villages often rely on either a part-time militia, or the local retainers of the manorial lord to perform law enforcement functions. Large town and especially cities, however, have evolved large, standing forces to keep the peace. In these urban centers, there are usually several barracks, spread throughout the city, where local guard units reside and train.\n\nGuardsmen tend to live modestly but comfortably, as most city administrations recognize the wisdom of a relatively contented protection force. With power comes temptation, however, and almost all city garrisons have trouble with corruption. When gold flows freely in the marketplaces, why settle for silver? Guilds of all types like to keep at least a few guardsmen on the payroll just in case, and any self-respecting crime lord will have a few officers of the Watch in his pocket.\n\nThey tend to live in communal barracks, with perhaps a few private rooms for officers. Obviously this life lends itself more to those without families, but in some cultures it is normal even for married guardsmen to live at least part-time in garrison with their shield mates. The conditions in these garrison barracks will vary, but there is limited privacy and constant motion and noise.\n\nAlthough the quality of the local City Watch will vary with location (and with the wealth of the community in question), in general guardsmen tend to be at the bottom end of the martial food chain, competent to corral drunks or deter petty thievery, but generally individually unable to stand up to an enraged barbarian or offended adventurer. Of course, the Watch isn’t expected to fight fair, and there are usually a lot of them, so adventuring parties would do well to remember that these women and men carry with them the full weight of the law of the city and of the realm. While defeating them in combat might not prove much of a challenge, escaping the long arm of retribution might…\n\n'}

{'header': '## Variations on the theme', 'content': '\nIn some cities, garrisons may also serve as central civic administration buildings. These structures will be more elaborate, encompassing office space for the local bureaucracy, and will tend to be less austere. Citizens seeking services may have to convince the local guards of their need before being allowed inside. This arrangement is particularly convenient for the local administration, as it improves the security of their important papers, seals, records, etc.\n\nIn some campaigns, the law is kept by garrisons headed by nobles, knights, or Paladins dedicated to the upholding of law and justice. These garrisons will obviously be more effective, having better morale and being generally well led.\n\nThe quality of the law enforcement in a city often depends upon the quality of the laws. In some cities, the garrisons will be made up of fanatics, religious or otherwise, whose sole goal in life to make sure that everyone conforms to a very strict code of conduct. Unwary travelers entering a city with odd and vigorously enforced bylaws, may soon find themselves on the wrong end of an encounter with the Watch.\n\n'}
